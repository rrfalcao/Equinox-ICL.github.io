Fifteen years bringing light to rural Rwanda.

Student-led · Engineer-built · Community-owned

Our mission

We uplift rural Rwandan communities through sustainable, self-sufficient, scalable engineering.

e.quinox is a student-led humanitarian organisation founded at Imperial College London. We bridge the gap between NGOs and industrial practice, fostering collaboration in the fields of sustainable energy and international development.

Through first-hand work with the people of Rwanda, we understand the wants and needs of the locals and how we can work together to advance their lifestyle. This is how we have successfully developed and delivered lasting projects on the ground for over fifteen years.

Every project is engineered at Imperial, tested by students on expedition, and handed over to Rwandan partners who run and maintain it long after our teams fly home.

2009

First expedition

Energy Kiosk

e.quinox launched its first pilot Energy Kiosk in Rwanda, marking the organisation's first expedition on the ground. The kiosk was monitored remotely through a custom data-logging system and maintained by Rwandan partners — keeping it running long after the team returned to London.

2013

Power to the home

Home Lighting

Four years later, e.quinox returned with portable battery boxes — giving households access to reliable light and power for the first time. Local entrepreneurs were trained to manufacture and distribute the systems: engineering solutions combined with community-led development.

2024

From electricity to water

Rainwater Recon

The 2024 team travelled to Rwanda to pilot a rainwater harvesting system for rural homes — scalable, accessible, transportable. They received guidance from Rwanda's Ministry of Infrastructure and the Dean of Engineering at the University of Rwanda.

Featured expedition

The pivot to water.

2024 marked the first expedition where e.quinox moved beyond electricity. Working with the Ministry of Infrastructure and the University of Rwanda, the team brought rainwater harvesting to homes in rural communities — scalable, accessible, transportable.
